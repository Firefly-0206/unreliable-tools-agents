\subsubsection{As-if descriptions: altered fault odds, losses or prior (post hoc)}\label{app:asif}
% src: raea4/agentenv/z_asif.py, z_asif_soft.py -> results/z_asif.json|txt, z_asif_soft.json|txt; scripts/make_z_outputs.py
This analysis was done after the results of \cref{app:x,app:y} were known, on their logs, with no plan fixed in advance and no model call. It asks whether one parameter can describe the under-use.
\emph{As-if planner.} The planner is exact but pessimistic about the replica: it multiplies the replica's fault odds by $g\ge1$, $\varphi'=g\varphi/(1-\varphi+g\varphi)$, and is otherwise the true model ($g=1$ is the oracle). With $g=4$ it opens with the replica tool in 100, 4, 0 and 0\% of persistent-fault episodes at $\varphi=0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7$, against 100, 100, 44 and 0\% for the oracle.
\emph{Deterministic rule.} For each run, $g$ is chosen on the persistent arm of the sweep (100 episodes) to maximize the number of episodes whose first-call choice (replica tool or not) agrees with the run's, and is then applied unchanged elsewhere (\cref{tab:asif}). For terra, $g=\zTerraG$ agrees in \zTerraMatchFit\% of persistent-fault and \zTerraMatchIndep\% of independent-fault episodes and with the majority of five samples in \zTerraMatchRep\% of the repeated instances, and the rule's regret, which is not fitted, is \zTerraRegretAsif\ against terra's \zTerraRegretObs; on the randomized surface agreement falls to \zTerraMatchRand\% (oracle \zTerraOracleMatchRand\%). For Sonnet the best $g$ (\zSonnetG) is barely better than ``never the replica tool'' on the persistent arm and worse than the oracle on the independent arm; for flash the rule degenerates to ``never''. A deterministic threshold describes terra's step-like switch, not Sonnet's graded choices.
\emph{Noise against pessimism.} The first action is one of five options (four tools, or submitting). We compare $P(a)\propto\exp(-Q^*(a)/\tau_0)$ on the true action values (noise only, one parameter) with $P(a)\propto\exp(-Q_g(a)/\tau)$ on the pessimistic planner's values (two parameters), both fitted by maximum likelihood on the persistent arm and evaluated unchanged on the independent arm, the randomized-surface episodes and the four further samples of the repeated instances (\cref{tab:asif}; a uniform choice has log-likelihood $-1.61$). For every run the tested noise-only model predicts the persistent-arm choices and their repeated samples less well than pessimism plus noise; other forms of decision noise were not tested. The advantage carries over to independent faults for terra and flash but not for Sonnet (\zSonnetLLPessIndep\ against \zSonnetLLNoiseIndep), in line with Sonnet's lower first-call share under persistence (\cref{app:x}), and not to the randomized surface for terra (\zTerraLLPessRand\ against \zTerraLLNoiseRand), in line with the surface effect of \cref{app:y}.

\emph{Altered stakes or prior (post hoc).} The same fitting rule was applied to as-if planners that keep the true fault model and change one stated quantity instead: the severe (false-benign) loss ($\times0.25$--$8$), the wrong-verdict loss ($\times0.5$--$3$), the prior odds of the benign state ($\times\frac18$--$8$), the escalation loss ($\times0.25$--$2.5$), every loss ($\times0.25$--$8$, equivalently every query cost and the budget divided by the factor), and a joint grid of severe and escalation loss (\cref{tab:asif-loss}). No value of the severe loss, the wrong-verdict loss or the prior lowers the replica first-call share at $\varphi=0.1$ and $0.3$ below \zlMinFirstLow\%, so these planners agree with terra's first-call choice in at most \zlTerraStakeMax\% and with Sonnet's in at most \zlSonnetStakeMax\% of persistent-arm episodes (fault odds: \zTerraMatchFit\% and \zSonnetMatchFit\%). A cheap escalation comes closer (terra \zlTerraEscFit\%, Sonnet \zlSonnetEscFit\%) only by hardly querying: \zlTerraEscCalls\ and \zlSonnetEscCalls\ calls per episode and \zlTerraEscEsc\% and \zlSonnetEscEsc\% escalations, against the models' \zlTerraObsCalls\ and \zlSonnetObsCalls\ calls and \zlTerraObsEsc\% and \zlSonnetObsEsc\% escalations, with regret \zlTerraEscRegret\ and \zlSonnetEscRegret\ against the models' \zlTerraObsRegret\ and \zlSonnetObsRegret. A uniform loss scale behaves the same way, and the joint grid does not exceed the fault-odds fit (\zlTerraJointFit\% and \zlSonnetJointFit\%). On the matched fault-free tasks (MZ; \cref{app:m}) the fitted variants that keep querying call tool 0 at least \zlTerraMZRepMin\ times per episode (oracle \zlOracleMZRep), against terra's \zlTerraMZRepObs\ and Sonnet's \zlSonnetMZRepObs. Within these families, a different reading of the stated losses, escalation cost or prior does not reproduce the first calls without contradicting the call and escalation counts.
% src: raea4/agentenv/r_robust.py -> results/r_robust.json|txt; scripts/make_u_outputs.py
\emph{Uncertainty about the documented fault rate (post hoc).} With a prior over $\varphi$, a planner acts in the persistent arm exactly as the oracle with $\varphi$ replaced by the prior mean: reports depend on $\varphi$ only through the session's stale flag, whose probability is that mean. A prior centered on the documented rate therefore changes no decision. An ambiguity-averse planner \citep{ellsberg1961risk} that plans for the upper end $\varphi+\delta$ of an ambiguity set, fitted by the same rule, needs $\delta=\uRobTerraDelta$ for terra and $\uRobSonnetDelta$ for Sonnet (a documented 0.3 read as \uRobTerraPhiThree\ and \uRobSonnetPhiThree). It agrees with \uRobTerraFit\% and \uRobSonnetFit\% of first calls on the persistent arm (oracle \uRobTerraOracleFit\% and \uRobSonnetOracleFit\%), with \uRobTerraIndep\% and \uRobSonnetIndep\% on the independent arm (oracle \uRobTerraOracleIndep\% and \uRobSonnetOracleIndep\%), and with \uRobTerraRand\% and \uRobSonnetRand\% on the randomized surface (oracle \zTerraOracleMatchRand\% and \zSonnetOracleMatchRand\%). Like the fault-odds multiplier, it describes terra within the sweep but not across surfaces, and Sonnet not across fault types; it makes the under-use a cautious response only if the documented rate is read as understating the fault rate about twofold at $\varphi=0.3$.
\emph{Reading.} Within a setting the under-use is described by a pessimistic multiplier on the fault odds (4 for terra under both fits; 4 or 12 for Sonnet, depending on the fit); the multiplier is not a stable property of a model across fault types and surfaces (Sonnet across fault types, terra across surfaces). This is an as-if description fitted after the fact; \cref{app:w} tests it on new episodes with $g$ fixed in advance. It does not identify an internal computation; of the one-parameter distortions compared, altered stated losses and prior fit the first calls less well, and a cheaper escalation or a uniform loss scale matches them only by barely querying, which contradicts the observed call and escalation counts; distortions outside these families were not compared.
